# First-run failure analysis and a fresh-test plan

This is a retrospective diagnosis of `artifacts/run-20261102-a`, not a clean validation of any subsequent design. The cases below were inspected after seeing the first held-out results. The proposed changes are therefore hypotheses selected with access to those results; they require a new held-out evaluation. The evidence does not establish an intrinsic inability of language models to understand time.

## What the saved results establish

The selected development configuration was uniform knots with a 384-token **evidence** budget, selected across observed and forecast questions. On the 18 development forecast questions it answered 4 correctly, versus 5 for the question-only control. The selection metric combined both task types, so it did not establish forecast-question improvement on development data.

On 36 held-out ETTh1 forecast questions, the TimesFM capsule answered 10 correctly, the cheap-forecast capsule 9, the mismatched forecast capsule 8, and the question-only control 5. These are small counts. The paired TimesFM-minus-cheap accuracy difference was 1/36, with the recorded window-cluster bootstrap interval from -3/36 to 5/36. ETTh1 windows come from one underlying series, so treating separated windows as independent remains an approximation.

The direct decision made from the complete forecast median was correct on 13/36 TimesFM cases and 10/36 cheap-forecast cases. The TimesFM versus cheap mean absolute errors, each divided by the context's standard deviation and averaged over cases, were 0.769 versus 1.060. This measures forecast error, not language reasoning. For TimesFM, the direct and language-model outcomes overlap as follows:

| Complete median forecast | Capsule answer | Cases |
|---|---|---:|
| Correct | Correct | 4 |
| Correct | Incorrect | 9 |
| Incorrect | Correct | 6 |
| Incorrect | Incorrect | 17 |

The analogous counts for the cheap forecaster are 2, 8, 7 and 19. Thus a numerical forecast can be correct while its capsule answer is wrong, but the reverse also occurs. The latter does not show that the model repaired the forecast: it could use history, uncertainty bands, option priors, or chance. These comparisons are descriptive, not causal attribution.

For observed tasks, temporal statistics scored 28/36, raw observations 24/36, and the selected uniform-history capsule 23/36. The raw input is substantially longer, and statistics versus uniform is a representation comparison with different content, not a pure compression-only comparison. Uniform-384 retained **18–34 of 128 historical samples**, never the full history, in the 36 observed cases. In forecast cases it retained 8–14 historical knots and 5–8 forecast knots. Its forecast error is a maximum numerical interpolation error after formatting, not a bound on answer correctness or predictive truth.

Conditional single-token A–D scoring needs particular care. On the TimesFM forecast arm, the unrestricted top token equaled the selected answer letter in 16/36 cases; 20/36 top tokens were not A–D, even allowing an initial space. Agreement alone does not measure free-generation accuracy, and a non-letter top token can simply begin a valid explanation. The two free-generation demos are useful examples, not a representative evaluation.

## Individual cases: what can be distinguished

All option letters and knot values below are from the retained held-out records. Times are hours after the last observation.

1. **`heldout-etth1-011545`: forecast limitation is sufficient to explain the direct error.** The complete TimesFM median peaks at hour 7, in Q2, while the true future maximum is in Q1. The capsule selects Q2 as well. The displayed median peaks around hour 8, also Q2. The forecast itself points to the wrong block; this case does not isolate a language-model failure.
2. **`heldout-etth1-010558`: the compressed point forecast supports the correct block, yet the forced choice differs.** The complete median peaks at hour 11 in Q2, matching the true future. The displayed point value at hour 11 is 20.09; the maximum of the displayed piecewise-linear point curve in Q3 is about 18.56. The stated 0.484 maximum interpolation error cannot close that roughly 1.53 gap between Q2 and Q3 by itself. Nevertheless, the capsule chooses Q3. This is evidence of a mismatch between this displayed point forecast and the conditional choice, not proof of the model's internal reasoning: the bands, history and prompt could all affect it.
3. **`heldout-etth1-010756`: a correct direct forecast and wrong capsule choice do not isolate compression.** The complete point median peaks at hour 24 in Q4, which is the true answer. The displayed points are 12.69 at hour 24 in Q4 and 12.32 at hour 16 in Q3; the capsule picks Q3. The 0.385 error bound is large relative to the 0.37 displayed difference, so the certificate alone does not preserve the block ranking. The full forecast shows what was lost or distorted by the interface, but does not identify whether the decisive cause was compression, bands, or answer selection.
4. **`heldout-etth1-012334`: small absolute error can still change a near-tie decision.** The complete median's largest value is 4.389 at hour 1 in Q1; the capsule includes 4.389 at hour 1 and 4.363 at hour 14 in Q3, only 0.026 apart, while the global error bound is 0.0603. It selects Q3; the true future answer is Q2. The point forecast itself misses the truth, and the small certificate does not preserve the ranking. Neither the wrong direct forecast nor the model's different choice isolates interpretation.
5. **`heldout-etth1-010361`: the free-text explanation demonstrates a concrete evidence-reading error.** The true future answer is Q4, and the demo answers Q4, but the complete forecast median peaks in Q3. Its explanation cites 16.9 at hour 19, which is an *upper quantile*, as the highest projected knot. The displayed upper quantile actually reaches 18.12 at hour 15 in Q3; it also calls 15.41 at hour -14 the highest observed knot despite a displayed 17.31 at hour -56. A correct final option here is not evidence of a correct reading of the forecast. This is one selected demo, not an estimated error rate.

These examples do not quantify how often each mechanism occurs. A rigorous decomposition would compare the full numerical forecast, a deterministic reconstruction of exactly the displayed median knots, a deterministic answer from the new structured representation, forced choice, and unconstrained text on the same cases.

## Predeclare a new evaluation before reading it

1. Keep all first-run files and this analysis as v1 exploratory evidence. Use only the existing ETTh1 development split to choose among a small fixed set of v2 encodings and prompt variants. Record every candidate, token counts, model versions, exact data fingerprints, and the selection rule; freeze them before evaluating new held-out data.
2. Compare the planned block summaries of point-median minimum and maximum with the existing knot representation. Keep marginal uncertainty bands visually separate, and include forecast-only versus history-plus-forecast as an ablation. Give the cheap model the identical representation and token-budget rules. Report actual full prompt tokens as well as evidence tokens; do not call independently tokenized evidence a total-prompt budget.
3. Before model scoring, mechanically compare each representation's reconstructed or explicitly summarized median block winner against the full forecast's block winner. Preserve hour boundaries and earliest-tie rules. This estimates answer changes caused by the representation; comparing that winner with the language-model choice measures a separate interface gap. Neither establishes why a choice changed.
4. On development data only, test an answer-prefix or other tokenization/scoring control alongside the existing forced-choice method. Require actual tokenizer IDs and consistent prompts across arms, and separately retain a bounded free-generation sample with explicit parsing. Select and freeze the scoring rule on development data; do not assume that first-token disagreement proves either scoring or generation is correct.
5. Evaluate the frozen design once on a separately acquired and fingerprinted **ETTh2** CSV and on fresh synthetic seeds and generator morphology. Maintain disjoint windows and keep each intervention pair in one cluster. ETTh2 is fresh to this local evaluation, but may have appeared in model pretraining; it is not guaranteed contamination-free. Report the synthetic and real-data results separately and limit confidence claims from one additional real series.
6. Report per-task counts, paired differences with cluster-aware uncertainty, direct forecast accuracy and error, representation fidelity, top-token agreement, actual prompt length and measured latency. Compare with question-only, raw, statistics, cheap forecast, TimesFM and a mismatched forecast. Interpret low power and dependence among windows explicitly. Repeatedly inspecting or changing this new held-out set makes it development data for the next iteration.
