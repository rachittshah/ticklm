# Research Report: Low-compute time-series foundation model and language model fusion

Generated: 2026-11-02. Scope: selected primary-source arXiv papers through 2026-11-02. This is targeted discovery, not an exhaustive or systematic review. Publication dates below are first arXiv submission dates; later versions are noted when material. Full text refers to selective inspection of the linked paper's text, not a complete methodological audit. Results are authors' claims, not independently replicated.

## Executive Summary

Time-series input to language models, soft-token connectors, TSFM-to-LLM alignment, specialist forecast aggregation, and local Apple MLX deployment are established ideas. TS-Reasoner connects frozen TimesFM to an LLM for reasoning but trains the language model; PocketTSLM runs a soft-prompt time-series language model on Apple devices; the 2026 OpenTSLM TeeMoE incorporates external forecasters. A defensible low-resource contribution would be an explicitly bounded empirical comparison of which time-series evidence representations improve held-out multi-step temporal reasoning, at what local cost, with both backbones frozen; even that is a hypothesis and not a global novelty claim.

## Research Question

What are the closest precedents for cheap, locally deployable TSFM and LLM fusion that improves temporal understanding beyond numerical forecasting? What experiment could distinguish actual contribution of the time-series representation from outsourcing, prompt artifacts, or shortcut learning?

## Key Findings

### Foundational distinctions and novelty

A forecaster predicts plausible future values conditioned on observed history; an event-time reasoner can also need temporal ordering, durations, changing conditions, multi-source alignment and decision constraints. A single forecast, regression result or threshold answer passed to an LLM may be useful tool augmentation, but successful verbatim transcription does not establish temporal understanding in the LLM. Conversely, a numerical specialist is a valid design component for a grounded system if the claim is about system-level behavior and experiments isolate what each component contributes. Fine-tuning a soft connector while leaving backbone weights frozen still changes system behavior; success does not prove that standalone frozen LLM weights acquired a capability.

"Frozen LLM" often means frozen base weights plus trainable LoRA adapters. PocketTSLM explicitly has per-task LoRA adapters (~1% of parameters each), plus encoder and projector; do not describe its whole LLM path as entirely unchanged.

### Recommended low-compute wedge

Test a query-independent, fixed-budget representation extracted from a frozen, pretrained TSFM — soft tokens, forecast distributions, or ordered evidence summaries — against a fixed frozen local LLM. Questions should require interpreting temporal facts alongside unrelated text or constraints, not just reporting a forecast. Benchmark both an untrained textual interface and a small trainable projector; consider no TSFM and randomized/swap controls. Compare representations on quality, latency, peak memory, context tokens, and training compute with matched prompts and input-information budgets. A meaningful improvement versus strong non-neural summaries and raw/text inputs, with preregistered split and paired confidence intervals, would support a narrow empirical claim; equal or worse results are informative too. Freezing weights is not a novelty claim: TEST uses frozen LLMs with learned time-series encoders and soft prompts, and TS-Reasoner uses a frozen pretrained TSFM.

Three possible connector routes: (1) verbatim/compressed text is immediately interpretable and cheap but loses fine structure and can leak answer; (2) trained continuous soft embeddings may retain latent structure and reduce input tokens but need alignment and risk instruction interference; (3) tool protocol with forecast distributions and event evidence supports audit but delegates numerical work and cannot by itself show intrinsic temporal knowledge. The design space should be tested, not settled in advance.

### Evaluation and falsification

- Establish question-only, raw values, downsampled values, deterministic statistics with temporal order, simple statistical forecast, TSFM forecast-only, TSFM evidence + LLM, and LLM with deliberately mismatched/shuffled TSFM evidence. Match relevant input budget and give baselines equivalent history where feasible.
- Ask multi-step questions whose answer is not already in the evidence: e.g. locate a historical regime change, compare before/after behavior with an external operational constraint, and choose an action while distinguishing observed history from uncertain projected future. Generate exact labels from ground truth, check for accidental shortcuts; provide an abstention option when the information cannot establish causation.
- Use paired counterfactuals: shift or reverse event order, timing, or relevant constraints; swap TSFM representations across examples while retaining prompts; measure how often answers and cited evidence change correctly. Include same-summary/different-order pairs to test whether merely supplying aggregates suffices.
- Split by underlying generated process family, seed and source series/domain rather than overlapping sliding windows; freeze decisions before held-out evaluation. Include independent real datasets if licensing and data leakage checks allow. For probabilistic forecasts test empirical calibration/coverage, rather than reporting model quantiles as reliable by default.
- Match model/hardware, prompt length where possible, and total cost; random or untrained encoders/projectors check whether pretrained temporal representations matter. Report training and inference costs, memory, multiple seeds and uncertainty. A tiny pilot does not establish generalization or real-world decision safety.

Authors report a dedicated encoder improves reasoning against textualization and progressive training improves data efficiency under their settings. It does not imply an untrained, both-frozen connector will work.

## Recommendations

Concentrate on evaluation design and cost rather than claiming an unprecedented fusion architecture. Separate a cheap tool-augmented baseline from a representation-alignment experiment, define hypotheses before final test, and explicitly report when the LLM merely repeats outputs. If the user seeks genuine broad temporal cognition, enlarge evaluation beyond forecasts and static pattern classification; this pilot alone cannot validate broad temporal understanding.

## Open Questions

- Is there already a TSFM-to-frozen-LLM projector trained exclusively on compact cross-task temporal supervision and evaluated under equal-cost counterfactuals? The targeted search does not establish an answer.
- Are the selected pretrained TSFM's internal representations accessible in the local runtime, and do they preserve temporal details needed for the tasks? This requires direct code and experiment verification.
- Are all benchmark source series cleanly held out from TSFM pretraining, question generation and model selection? This is often difficult to establish.
- Search covers targeted results available on 2026-11-02, not every arXiv paper in 2026. Some results, implementation details and metrics are only checked against abstracts.

## Focused prior-art check: history knots and multi-horizon forecast quantiles

Project hypothesis under investigation: a query-independent, fixed-budget textual capsule containing adaptive piecewise-linear knots from observed history and an explicitly time-indexed forecast-quantile summary, consumed by a frozen local LLM. The frozen TSFM and a cheap statistical forecaster can use the same codec. These are research design choices, not demonstrated properties of an implementation or evidence of novelty.

### Particularly close: textual TSFM quantiles supplied to an LLM

**Time Series Foundation Models Improve LLM Decisions: A Case Study in Stock Trading**, anonymous manuscript, ICLR 2026 Time Series in the Age of Large Models workshop, [full manuscript](https://openreview.net/pdf/9bb4107380968282f9c347f7a8e2592032d777fa.pdf). *Selected full text inspected; the manuscript says under review. The original submission date and final acceptance status were not independently verified.* Appendix A evaluates six textual representations of the same TSFM forecast; format 6 reports q05, q50 and q95 at several explicit horizons. Its prompt includes price history and forecasts, uses TimesFM and other TSFMs, and asks an LLM for portfolio decisions. The compressed multi-horizon quantile interface and TSFM-to-LLM decisions are therefore clear prior art. The paper presents predetermined horizon selection, not the proposed adaptive piecewise-linear historical knots; the exact combined codec and fixed-budget evaluations require further examination and should not be asserted unique. Its paper-specific stock-trading outcomes are not evidence for other tasks or real-world profit.

**LLM as Forecasting Planner: Training-Free Text Conditioning for Time-Series Foundation Models**, first submitted 2026-07-27, [arXiv:2607.24892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24892). *Abstract checked.* Frozen TSFM proposals and an LLM select trajectories using text; this is another training-free integration and quantitative forecasting use, though not evidence of identical capsule contents. **OpenTSLM TeeMoE**, 2026-09-30, [arXiv:2609.40265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40265), uses learned experts for temporal reasoning and forecast aggregation; external forecast synthesis is also established.

### Classical history compression

- Urs Ramer, **An iterative procedure for the polygonal approximation of plane curves** (1972), [original DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0146-664X%2872%2980017-0). *Bibliographic metadata checked through a published paper's references; original publisher full text not accessible in this search.*
- David H. Douglas and Thomas K. Peucker, **Algorithms for the reduction of the number of points required to represent a digitized line or its caricature** (1973), [original DOI](https://doi.org/10.3138/FM57-6770-U75U-7727). *Bibliographic metadata checked; original publisher full text not accessible in this search.* Recursive curve simplification is old prior art; the exact distance metric matters. A perpendicular distance threshold for plotted coordinates is not automatically a bound on vertical time-series interpolation error.
- Eamonn Keogh, Selina Chu, David Hart and Michael Pazzani, **An online algorithm for segmenting time series** (2001), [IEEE proceedings DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICDM.2001.989531). *IEEE metadata and abstract checked via search; full text not inspected.* The abstract reviews existing piecewise-linear time-series representations and algorithms. Adaptive segmentation of historical values is itself well-established.
- **Sensor Data Compression Using Bounded Error Piecewise Linear Approximation with Resolution Reduction** (2019), [publisher full text](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/12/13/2523). *Selected full text checked.* Explicitly formulates a maximum absolute vertical reconstruction-error bound on sampled time points. This is different from limiting token count: a fixed number of knots gives no universal fidelity guarantee unless the reconstruction error is measured and reported.

### Limits of the proposed representation

- Query-independent compression can discard the observation needed for a later question. Measure actual model-token counts and reconstruction error; identify which family of questions survives a given budget. Budgeting by knot count alone is not equivalent to budgeting by tokenizer tokens.
- Pointwise forecast quantiles are **marginal** distributions at each horizon. They do not, by themselves, specify a joint distribution across time or probabilities of paths, such as crossing a threshold at least once. Do not call a set of horizon-wise quantiles a calibrated joint trajectory distribution. Evaluate empirical coverage on held-out data before calling the intervals calibrated.
- Padding the LLM prompt with a detailed forecast may allow direct answer extraction or copying. Counterfactual swapping, same-codec statistical forecast, question-only and history-only controls, and questions involving ordering or external constraints can isolate the respective contributions. They cannot prove the frozen LLM weights gained a new temporal ability.
- Fixed-budget compression can lose local spikes, extrema and ordering, even when overall error is low. Assess both numerical distortion and downstream task performance under the **same token budget** as uniform sampling and simpler baselines. Keep observed history distinct from predicted values and avoid future-data leakage.

### Defensible claim boundary

A possible finding is that, on specified held-out tasks and hardware, a particular local, training-free codec provides a useful quality–latency–token trade-off against matched baselines. Adaptive knot allocation, query-independent prompt summaries, quantile tubes, and TSFM-to-LLM fusion all have related precedent; the literature search is not a systematic novelty or patent search. The outcome remains an empirical question.
