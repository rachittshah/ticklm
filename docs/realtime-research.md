# Research: A real-time temporal coprocessor for an LLM agent

Date: 2026-11-02. This note addresses continuously passing real time and changing external state. It does not treat static question-answering over a recorded time series as a demonstration of real-time awareness. The design below is a testable system hypothesis, not a claim of globally novel architecture or of changed model weights.

## What the literature already establishes

Selected primary sources; first submission dates are shown and later versions are called out. “Full text” means selected sections examined, not full independent replication. All reported metrics are the authors' claims under their experimental conditions.

1. **TimeInteract: Towards Real-Time Interactive Intelligence for Streaming Time Series**, 2026-09-22, [arXiv:2609.26389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26389). *Selected full text.* The closest task-level precedent: a model continuously consumes time-series chunks, learns response timing, and decouples ongoing control from response generation through separate key/value caches. The authors train the interaction system and unfreeze its LLM during stages; this is not the proposed unchanged-weights setup. Their StreamTSI-34K contains 34,588 episodes. Streaming observation and deciding when to respond are already published ideas.
2. **On the Clock: Towards Punctual and Productive Time-Budgeted AI Agents**, 2026-10-07, [arXiv:2610.10833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10833). *Selected full text.* The paper tests harness clock feedback, hard stop hooks and reinforcement training. A pull-only clock was often not consulted; automatic per-turn clock injection worked better for budget adherence. Authors also find punctuality does not guarantee productive use of time, and between-generation feedback cannot interrupt a long generation. A clock-aware, no-TSFM harness is therefore a required baseline.
3. **AgentTime: Can Agents Estimate and Control Their Own Runtime?**, 2026-10-07, v2 2026-10-08, [arXiv:2610.09944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09944). *Selected full text.* Benchmark of elapsed-time estimation, future runtime prediction and duration following across 222 tasks. Authors caution that meeting a requested duration by sleeping is not evidence of productive work. Its runtime methodology is directly relevant to testing active agents.
4. **Real-Time Deadlines Reveal Fragile Temporal Adaptation in LLM Strategic Dialogues**, 2026-01-19, v2 2026-08-30, [arXiv:2601.13206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13206). *Selected full text.* In simulated negotiations, per-turn remaining-time feedback improved closure for GPT-5.1-chat-latest from 4% to 32% in the authors' experiment. Their updated paper reports that time-feedback effects vary by model and presentation. This is behavioral evidence, not proof of a specific internal mechanism or a result for our model.
5. **Event-VStream: Event-Driven Real-Time Understanding for Long Video Streams**, 2026-01-22, [arXiv:2601.15655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15655). *Selected full text.* Motion, semantic change and predictive cues identify event boundaries; generation is triggered at boundaries and events stored in a persistent memory. Adaptive event-triggered model invocation and predictive cues already have direct precedent, albeit in video.
6. **Speculative Interaction Agents: Building Real-Time Agents with Asynchronous I/O and Speculative Tool Calling**, 2026-05-13, v2 2026-05-14, [arXiv:2605.13360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13360). *Abstract and section headings checked.* Decouples the agent from input waiting and explores speculative tool calls; authors also train clock-aware small edge models. Highlights that streaming input should continue during tool execution and generation, and that speculative results can be invalidated by later information.
7. **PhoStream: Benchmarking Real-World Streaming for Omnimodal Assistants in Mobile Scenarios**, 2026-01-30, [arXiv:2601.22575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22575). *Abstract checked.* Online audio/video benchmark; authors report that forward-looking questions can trigger premature responses before relevant information arrives. It motivates evaluating whether the agent speaks at the right moment, in addition to correctness. It uses LLM-as-judge for some evaluation, a limitation to address.
8. **Asynchronous Tool Usage for Real-Time Agents**, 2024-10-28, [arXiv:2410.21620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21620). *Abstract checked.* Event-driven finite-state machine and asynchronous tool execution to avoid a strictly sequential agent. Asynchronous agent scheduling itself predates the proposed system.

## Proposed system: precise clocks, observable state, predictive wakeups

A small external runtime gives a frozen LLM fresh temporal context and, optionally, a frozen time-series model's forecast. The LLM is invoked to reason or respond; a lightweight scheduler can continue to receive and record observations while it runs. This is **system-level time awareness**, not evidence of an intrinsic clock or new temporal abilities in the frozen LLM's parameters.

1. **Time source.** Read the operating system's clock. Wall-clock UTC timestamps identify calendar events; an elapsed-time clock measures intervals, timeouts and deadline budgets. Do not subtract raw readings from different clock domains. Validate local clock behavior across system sleep, suspend, process restart and wall-clock adjustment; monotonic-clock sleep behavior is platform-specific. On restart, establish a fresh clock anchor and do not compare monotonic readings from incompatible sessions. Calendar time can jump or be wrong; label source and uncertainty when synchronization is unknown.
2. **Event/state contract.** Every observation carries a source ID, event ID/version, event-time timestamp if available, local receive time, value, and observation provenance. Use local elapsed time for age since receipt; event-time age is only meaningful with compatible/synchronized clocks and should otherwise be marked unknown. Update state idempotently by explicit IDs, handle duplicate or late/out-of-order messages, and record gaps. Carry an explicit expiry/max-age and deadline on each task. A stale or unavailable observation becomes unknown, not implicitly current.
3. **Snapshot and response validity.** Generate from an immutable versioned snapshot including current wall time, elapsed time, age of each material fact, task deadline and intervening events. Continue ingestion during model generation. Before delivering an action or asserting that a state is current, compare the snapshot version and deadline with the current one; re-evaluate, qualify or discard stale output. This requires a real asynchronous pipeline: claiming concurrency from sequential callbacks is insufficient.
4. **Adaptive wake decision.** Deterministic task deadlines, data-freshness limits, explicit new user input and observed critical events take priority. The TSFM is an optional scheduler adviser: given a recorded, regularly sampled numeric stream, it predicts changes or likely threshold proximity. Wake earlier when predicted risk is high; where predictions are absent, stale, invalid, or outside observed data, fall back to a simple bounded periodic schedule or deterministic event trigger. Do not call a quantile curve a first-passage probability; marginal per-horizon quantiles do not determine the joint probability of a crossing. The actual threshold crossing is known only on observation.
5. **One possible wake rule.** Let now be m, a monotonic deadline d, maximum allowed data age A, last local receive time m_obs, maximum periodic delay P, and prediction-suggested delay h_pred (all durations in the same elapsed-time domain). Schedule the next *planned inspection* no later than min(m + P, m_obs + A, d - reserve, m + h_pred), clamping elapsed or invalid terms to an immediate inspection. New observations or user input may wake the loop sooner. This is a best-effort scheduling policy, not a hard real-time guarantee. Reserve must include observed LLM, I/O and cancellation costs, and no fixed reservation proves a deadline can be met on an ordinary operating system.

The TSFM does not measure elapsed time, guarantee a deadline, or create a clock inside the LLM. Fixed sampling, timestamps, hard expiration and clock-only prompting can deliver useful behavior without it. Predictor advice is justified only if it improves an observed timing/cost trade-off over those simpler controls.

## Experiments that test the actual claim

Run an actual local live event producer and actual model, with logged event/receive/start/end times and stable run IDs. A deterministic replay is useful for exact comparisons but should be named replay, not proof of continuous live operation. Separate scheduling/clock tests from LLM comprehension and predictor experiments.

**Baselines:** (a) frozen LLM with only initial task/time, (b) frozen LLM with fresh clock/deadline each turn, (c) (b) plus deterministic fixed-period/freshness/event scheduling, (d) (c) plus cheap trend/seasonal predictor, and (e) (c) plus frozen TSFM. Match event trace and measure all model/scheduler calls and cold/warm cost. An oracle with full future may be shown only as a nondeployable bound. Clock refresh plus deadline enforcement is an essential positive control.

**Live scenarios:** (1) an actual elapsed-time question after an idle interval; verify elapsed time from the OS, not a model guess; (2) a sensor-like stream with a real future threshold event, user-defined deadline, and data timestamps, requiring an early warning if justified and an observation-confirmed update after crossing; (3) a changed or withdrawn fact arriving during slow generation, then verify the stale output is intercepted or explicitly marked; (4) an expiry/missing update with a no-longer-current state, duplicates and reordered events; (5) a wall-clock adjustment or injected clock abstraction and a restart/suspend, where available, checking clock-domain semantics.

**Outcomes:** report elapsed-time error, stale-as-current rate, deadline-miss rate, event recall, warning lead time, false/early warning rate, event-to-action p50/p95 latency, LLM and TSFM calls, real CPU/GPU wall time, and cost per useful action. Record sample rates and time spent blocked. For each important result retain a full trace showing when data was actually available and when the decision was generated. Evaluate repeated paired traces, including sudden unforecastable jumps, regime shifts, quiet periods and missing sources. Avoid inferring future-event quality from only an ordinary linear trend. Test for data leakage from simulated future generator state.

A successful pilot might show that the full system notices actual time and fresh changes, behaves correctly on deadline/staleness interventions, and uses fewer resources for the same event recall/latency than the strongest clock-only or cheap-predictor control. If TSFM advice fails to improve that frontier, use the simpler scheduler and report a negative TSFM result. Tiny traces establish feasibility only, not reliability in real-world operations. The unchanged LLM alone has not acquired an intrinsic sense of time.

## Open questions

- Which local OS clock includes suspended time, and how do its documented semantics and measured behavior relate to the user's intended elapsed-time definition?
- Which real observation source and interaction does the user intend? A local synthetic live stream exercises the machinery but does not establish utility for the user's actual environment.
- Can the TSFM forecast fast enough to be useful relative to the stream's sampling period, LLM latency and deadline? Measure its full online invocation and synchronization cost.
- How should the scheduler prioritize fresh data and deadlines when many independent streams exceed compute capacity? A single-stream demonstration does not establish multi-stream scalability.
